ENTRY NO. 003 Week Three

Keep the Main Thing the Main Thing

I've recently been blessed with an opportunity to start a new chapter in my life.

It's a different direction for me, and I'm excited about the transition. For a long time, so much of my life revolved around one goal. Now I have an opportunity to take that same work ethic, discipline, competitiveness, and consistency and apply it somewhere new.

I'm walking into this chapter with a lot to learn, but also a lot to prove to myself.

One thing I've been thinking about lately is keeping the main thing the main thing.

There will always be distractions. There will always be something that can take my attention away from what actually matters. Things that feel important in the moment but don't move me any closer to where I'm trying to go.

At this point in my life, I want to eliminate as much of that noise as possible.

My focus is simple: show up, learn, work, improve, and take advantage of the opportunity in front of me.

I don't need to have everything figured out right now. I just need to make sure my actions are aligned with what I say I want.

That means being disciplined with my time.

Being intentional about who and what gets my attention.

Preparing even when nobody is watching.

And understanding that every day doesn't have to feel exciting to be productive.

I'm excited because this transition gives me a chance to build something new. I get to be a beginner again. I get to learn a new game, develop new skills, meet new people, and see how far I can take it.

But excitement alone isn't enough.

Now it's time to lock in.

No unnecessary distractions. No losing sight of the bigger picture. No letting temporary things interfere with long-term goals.

There's an opportunity in front of me, and I want to give it my full attention.

Keep the main thing the main thing. Everything else is noise.

— DJ

ENTRY NO. 002 Week Two

Entering a Different World

Last week closed out with some good news — a new opportunity I've been building toward. But that's not really what this entry is about. It's about something bigger: what it actually feels like to walk out of one world, the only one you've ever known, and into a completely different one.

Being an athlete can make you an introvert to everyone who isn't one. You live inside a totally different rhythm for most of your life — different rooms, different pressure, different people — and when that ends, the outside world can feel like it's moving at a different speed. For me, that transition has actually been going well. My mental state is in a better place without football than it was with it, and honestly, I think I knew that the moment my last NFL game ended.

What's helped most is accepting things — the good, the bad, and whatever the outcome turns out to be. I've lived by one line for a long time: if you can look in your own mirror and honestly say you gave everything you had, there's no room left for regret. Showing up and executing, every single day, is what clears that out.

— DJ

ENTRY NO. 001 Week One

Why I Traded the Field for the Grind

People assume that once you've made it to the NFL, even for a preseason, the hard part is over. It isn't. The hard part is what you do the day nobody's handing you a jersey anymore.

I spent years building the kind of work ethic that gets a kid from Pop Warner to a preseason roster — through setbacks that would've ended it for a lot of people. That work ethic didn't retire when football did. It just needed a new field. Right now that field is sales, and eventually it's a business that's fully mine.

This journal is me writing it down as it happens — the wins, the weeks that don't go how I planned, and the discipline it takes to keep showing up anyway. If you're building something of your own, I think you'll recognize a lot of it.

— DJ
